Set the location from Google Maps

  1. Open Google Maps and find the spot you want to stand on.
  2. On a computer: right-click the spot. The first line of the menu is its latitude and longitude; click it to copy them.
    On a phone: press and hold the spot to drop a pin, then copy the coordinates shown for the pin.
  3. Paste them below. The full link from the address bar works too, once a pin is dropped.

Links from the Share button (maps.app.goo.gl/...) can't be read from here, so copy the coordinates instead.

The seasonal sky viewer

Stand in a circle of stones and watch the sky the way people who built stone circles did: where the sun and moon rise and set through the year, and the turning points they marked: the solstices and equinoxes, the four fire festivals (Samhain, Imbolc, Bealtaine and Lúnasa) and the moon's long 18.6-year cycle of standstills.

Everything is worked out for the place and moment you choose: the sun, the moon and its phase, the stars and the Milky Way, and even the colour of the ground through the seasons.

The arcs across the sky

Each arc is the path the sun or moon takes across the sky on a particular day; where it meets the horizon, a marker shows the rising or setting point.

Hover over or tap a marker's label for the next date of what it marks, the sunrise or sunset time that day, and its compass bearing, so you can find the direction outside with a phone's compass. Tap anywhere else to close it.

Looking around

  • Turn with the left / right arrow keys, and walk with up / down (or use the arrows on screen).
  • Look around by dragging with the right mouse button, or dragging with a finger.
  • Reset takes you back to the centre of the circle, facing south.

Choosing a place and time

  • Set the latitude and longitude in the Observer panel, or use How to get your lat / long easily to copy them from Google Maps. The viewer remembers the last place.
  • Set the date and time. The time zone is worked out from the place (Auto), or choose one yourself.
  • The Sun and Moon checkboxes show or hide their arcs, and the small arrow buttons fold the panels away.
  • The page's address follows the place (and the date and time, once you set them), so it can be bookmarked or shared; Copy a link to this place and time copies one that opens the viewer at the same place and moment.

Bearings are from true north, and rise / set times assume a level horizon: hills delay a sunrise and move it a little. The fire festivals are taken as the points halfway between the solstices and equinoxes, as opposed to their traditional 1st day of the Gregorian calendar month. You can always update the Observer dates to the 1st of the month to see the Sun and Moon locations for the traditional Fire Festival dates.
